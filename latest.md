# News Digest — 18 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Nvidia beat expectations and raised its outlook, and its shares rose after hours. Its chairman also ruled out bringing Jensen Huang back as CEO and promised a fast search for a new boss. Asian stocks look set to end their losing streak on the back of it. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
2. Before those results, it was a rough day: Europe's stocks had their worst day in eight months, the FTSE 100 too, and Wall Street closed lower, all on nerves about AI. Attention now turns to Anthropic's stock market debut. (Reuters)
3. Bitcoin is stuck near 64 thousand dollars. Market maker Wintermute warns it could slip into the 50 thousands as traders bet the Fed may raise rates. (CoinDesk)
4. Fed official Alberto Musalem says the US probably needs several rate hikes by spring to bring inflation under control. Meanwhile, US retail sales held up well. (Reuters)
5. Ukraine struck the Crimean bridge for a second day, in what Russia calls the biggest drone attack of the war, just before Putin meets Xi and Trump. (Reuters, Delfi)
6. The European Commission has cut its forecast for Lithuania's economic growth to 2.7 percent and raised its inflation forecast to 5.1 percent. Lithuania's banking association also says the country won't avoid an EU excessive deficit procedure. (LRT)
7. The EU has signed off Lithuania's final recovery fund payment, worth 993 million euros. (The Brussels Times)
8. Lithuania's teachers' strike goes on, with about four thousand teachers off work. The Seimas education committee is holding an emergency meeting on Friday. (Delfi, LRT)
9. The Trump administration is pushing ahead with rules to stop China using American AI data centres, despite lobbying from Amazon, Google and Meta. (Reuters)
10. Norway's oil giant Equinor says it's dealing with a cyberattack on some IT systems, though oil and gas output isn't affected. (Reuters)

## Lithuania

- With US armoured troops back in the country, Lithuania is pushing for a permanent American base. President Nausėda says the rotations will continue. (Stars and Stripes, Military Times, LRT)
- The chief of defence, Raimundas Vaikšnoras, suggests building physical barriers on the border with Russia's Kaliningrad region. He also warned that radar can mistake drones, and says the whole country can't be covered by an iron dome. (LRT, Delfi)
- Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys says Vilnius won't budge on Belarus, which he calls "absolutely complicit". Lithuania is also seeking more than 200 million euros from Minsk over the migration crisis. (The Kyiv Independent, Belsat)
- President Nausėda's adviser says the president supports limiting the number of migrant workers, and Lithuania won't accept any migrants under the EU solidarity quota in 2027. Meanwhile, the transport industry wants higher quotas for foreign drivers. (Delfi, LRT)
- The government is tightening control of ride-hailing drivers, and about five thousand of them could lose their permits to work. (LRT)
- Seimas Speaker backs a tax on luxury cars, and the government is also raising gambling taxes to bring in about 36 million euros. (Delfi, Gaming Intelligence)
- A deputy minister says a windfall tax would be easier to set if Orlen hands over its financial reports. (LRT)
- The economy minister says the value of investment projects this year is approaching one billion euros. (LRT)
- From next year, the rules for personal bankruptcy are changing. (Delfi)
- Grid operator Litgrid has added 168 megawatts of battery storage. And Westinghouse, which is dismantling the Ignalina nuclear plant's drums, has opened a Lithuanian branch. (CEEnergynews, 15min)
- Lithuania dropped to 18th place in the EU's gender equality index, with politics and business the weakest areas. (LRT)
- A volunteer soldier has died, and military police are investigating. (Delfi, 15min)
- In business, the Akropolis ice rink in Vilnius is closing for good, and the dating site One.lt is shutting down. (15min, LRT)
