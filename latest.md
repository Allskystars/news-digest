# News Digest — 19 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Anthropic has priced the biggest stock market listing ever, at 83 dollars a share, raising around 81 billion dollars ahead of its Nasdaq debut. Stocks are rising in anticipation, and options on the shares could start trading as early as Monday. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
2. The Fed is leaning towards raising interest rates. Minutes from the October meeting show some officials wanted a bigger hike, Governor Chris Waller is again calling for a half-point increase in December, and Michael Barr says rate rises likely lie ahead. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
3. Bitcoin slipped below 63 thousand dollars as the Anthropic listing drew attention and money, though analysts say blaming the IPO alone is too simple. Gold also fell about 2 percent ahead of the likely Fed hike. (CoinDesk)
4. Wall Street ended mixed after Nvidia's strong earnings, as investors still fret about who will lead the company after Jensen Huang. The US dollar is hovering near a 19-month high. (Reuters)
5. Lithuania's Seimas holds its first debate today on the 2027 state budget, with the ruling coalition still arguing over family policy and pay for officers. The opposition-leaning "farmers" party says it doesn't yet know how it will vote. (15min, LRT)
6. The IMF is urging Lithuania to adopt a credible medium-term fiscal plan as debt risks grow, even though it says growth remains robust. On top of that, Eurostat data show Lithuania had the second-highest annual inflation in the EU in October. (LRT, Delfi)
7. UniCredit and Germany have reached a broad agreement on UniCredit's takeover of Commerzbank, according to a Reuters exclusive. (Reuters)
8. UK inflation rose to a one-year high, and the Bank of England says it will rise further, though markets actually trimmed their bets on a rate hike. (Reuters)
9. Trump, Xi and Putin are meeting in China, with Trump calling the summit a win before it even began. Lithuania's President Nausėda, meanwhile, heads to Berlin for a meeting of Ukraine's European allies. (Reuters, LRT, 15min)
10. The IMF's chief says global inflation means many economies need to tighten monetary policy. Separately, J.P. Morgan warns that developed-country budget deficits are at their highest since World War Two outside of recessions. (Reuters)

## Lithuania

- The 2027 budget gets its first reading in the Seimas today, after another round of coalition council talks. Finance Minister Taurimas Valys says a proposed windfall tax on fuel producers wouldn't target just one company — widely read as Orlen Lietuva. (15min, LRT)
- Teachers say they'll keep striking after a meeting with Education Minister Raminta Popovienė solved nothing. The minister also visited a striking school in Nemenčinė, where she faced tough questions. (LRT, 15min, Delfi)
- Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys says Lithuania won't ease pressure on Belarus, and warned that negotiating about anything other than Russia's aggression is a trap. (Belsat, uatv)
- The border with Belarus has been quiet, with no attempts at illegal crossings for three days in a row. Separately, officials are warning about a hostile influence operation stoking fears over the border and smuggling. (Delfi, 15min)
- The National Audit Office warns that if the army's needs aren't met, Lithuania risks not fully building its national division by 2030. (LRT, Delfi)
- Lithuania is offering US defence start-up Mach Industries fast-track conditions to invest in the country. (LRT)
- The family referendum has stalled, with organisers blaming the electoral commission, and the minister says it most likely won't be held together with next year's local elections. (LRT)
- Minister Katelynas rejects accusations of xenophobia after Lithuania refused to take in migrants under the EU's relocation scheme. (LRT)
- The Bank of Lithuania fined two electronic money institutions for breaches, and a court backed the central bank's instructions to Paysera on how it invests client money. (15min, LRT)
- The government backed cutting paperwork in public procurement and transferring the new children's hospital building to Santaros Clinics. (15min, LRT, Delfi)
- The US Pentagon says its troop deployment in Lithuania will be extended. (Daily Asian Age)
- The president has signed a law widening access to assisted reproduction. (LRT)
