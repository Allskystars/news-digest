# News Digest — 24 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Jensen Huang is back as Nvidia's CEO after the board reversed itself following an intervention by President Trump. Nvidia shares jumped by about 30 percent, and the Nasdaq gained more than 6 percent in one day. (Reuters, CoinDesk, LRT)
2. The AI rally spread around the world. Europe's STOXX index had its best day in more than six months, and Asian stocks rallied again overnight. (Reuters)
3. The dollar climbed as the renewed AI excitement brought back bets on Fed rate hikes. New York Fed President John Williams says inflation is still too high and more hikes are possible. (Reuters)
4. CrowdStrike is buying fellow cybersecurity firm Zscaler in a 44-billion-dollar deal. (Reuters)
5. Bitcoin bounced back above 61,000 dollars after a weekend plunge to about 55,500, its lowest in more than two years. But it lagged far behind tech stocks and was already losing steam by the evening. (CoinDesk)
6. The US has filed a court action to recover more than 1 billion dollars in cryptocurrency stolen in the Binance hack. Researchers say the hackers have already pushed 247 million dollars through mixing transactions to hide where it came from. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
7. Lithuania's leaders flatly deny any involvement in the reported drone attack on Kaliningrad. President Nausėda called it a bubble out of nothing, and Foreign Minister Budrys called it Russian disinformation. (LRT, Delfi, Lrytas)
8. The Lithuanian teachers' strike may be nearing an end. Union leader Andrius Navickas hopes to reach a deal with the prime minister on Tuesday, and President Nausėda is urging both sides to settle because pupils are suffering. (LRT, 15min)
9. The EU has proposed its 22nd sanctions package against Russia, including measures on banks, a visa ban for Russian soldiers and a ban on Russian athletes at European events. (Reuters, LRT, 15min)
10. Euro zone growth held steady in November, but France's deepening downturn is clouding the outlook. German business activity, on the other hand, picked up speed. (Reuters)

## Lithuania

- Teachers' strike: the prime minister is meeting education unions about teachers' pay. President Nausėda says long strikes hurt pupils and calls for clarity on how pay is calculated. (15min, LRT)
- Finance Minister Mindaugas Sinkevičius admits the budget deficit isn't what he'd like it to be. One economist warns that falling oil prices are actually bad news for state revenue. (LRT)
- Politicians are debating whether unemployment benefits, which cost more than 600 million euros a year, have become too generous, and some want stricter rules. (LRT)
- Lithuania is pushing back against EU budget cuts, saying the eastern flank has to be a priority. (LRT)
- Defence: the army's HIMARS rocket launchers will arrive late, in the first quarter of next year. The army chief says other equipment would make up for delayed weapons in the new division. (Delfi, 15min)
- Lithuania marked Armed Forces Day with a parade in Vilnius and fighter jets flying over the capital. Two colonels were promoted to brigadier general. (LRT, Delfi)
- Lithuania, together with three other EU countries, plans a joint investigation into arson attacks linked to Starlink. (LRT, 15min)
- Aurelijus Veryga wants a guarantee that the referendum on the family can be held together with the Seimas elections. President Nausėda says it's important to ask the people, and it shouldn't be tied to the budget. (LRT)
- Poland's energy minister says now is not the right time to sell Orlen Lietuva. (LRT)
- Energy Minister Lukas Savickas is urging the US to help Europe give up all Russian energy. He's also meeting his Norwegian counterpart about energy security. (Energy Ministry, 15min)
- The Lithuanians who had been detained in the Democratic Republic of Congo are back home. Officials say they were simply tourists. (LRT)
- Gamblers in Lithuania lost 229 million euros in the first nine months of the year, and online gambling now makes up about 78 percent of the market. (LRT, iGaming Business)
- A big fire destroyed the Virkštininkai manor in Kretinga district, run by chef Rokas Galvonas. Investigators suspect an electrical fault. (LRT, Delfi, 15min)
- The US ambassador is saying goodbye to Lithuania, and also reminded the public about political prisoners in Belarus. (Delfi, REFORM.news)

## Markets & Economy

- Wall Street rebounded strongly on Huang's return. Nvidia short sellers took a hit of about 12 billion dollars, and the rally is set to add about 2 trillion dollars to the Nasdaq-100. (Reuters)
- Morgan Stanley is now the most bullish on stocks it has been since April, after last week's sell-off. Hedge funds, though, just had their worst week since April 2025. (Reuters)
- Gold edged higher as bargain hunters stepped in after a steep weekly drop. Investors are waiting for US inflation data. (Reuters)
- US business activity growth slowed further in November, a survey shows. (Reuters)
- ECB policymaker Gediminas Šimkus, head of Lithuania's central bank, says a December rate hike might not be enough, but there's no rush to move again after that. (Reuters)
- Lithuanian bank Citadele cut its forecast for Lithuania's growth by 0.2 points, to 2.7 percent. Its chief says Lithuanians remain the most optimistic people and businesses in the Baltics. (LRT, Delfi)
- Electricity prices in Lithuania fell 30 percent in a week, according to grid operator Litgrid. (LRT)
- Lithuanian firm Brolis Semiconductors won an order from the Swedish armed forces worth about 14 million euros. Urbo bank's profit grew 39 percent this year, to 6.1 million euros. (LRT)
- About 290 million euros in EU support is about to be handed out in Lithuania. (15min)
- In corporate deals: ABB is buying Germany's Ziehl-Abegg for 2.65 billion dollars, Lego will invest about 1 billion euros in a new factory in Portugal, and Eli Lilly is buying a Huntington's disease drug developer for up to 725 million dollars. (Reuters)
- In the UK, Capita's pension problems forced out its CEO and led to a cash call, and its shares fell to a 35-year low. (Reuters)
- Congo has halted cobalt exports again to tackle oversupply and support prices. (Reuters)
- Macron wants to revive a plan to write fiscal rules into the French constitution. France's minister warns that next year's 1 percent growth forecast may be cut. (Reuters)
