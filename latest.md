# News Digest — 17 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Wall Street closed sharply lower on Monday, with the Nasdaq down about two and a half percent. Investors are dumping AI stocks ahead of Nvidia's results this week and Anthropic's upcoming share listing, and Asian markets followed them down this morning. (Reuters)
2. Bitcoin is struggling around 64 thousand dollars after dropping to its lowest level since July on Sunday. The most popular options bet now is on a fall below 60 thousand, not a rally to 100 thousand. (CoinDesk)
3. Gold has sunk to a one-year low, because rising bond yields and a stronger dollar are pulling money away from it. It's on track for its worst year since 2015. (Reuters)
4. A new rotation of American troops was formally welcomed in Pabradė. President Nausėda says the rotations will continue after this one, and Lithuania has put forward a proposal for a permanent US presence. (Reuters, LRT, Anadolu Agency)
5. After Estonia announced it will close its border with Russia, President Nausėda says Lithuania could do the same if the sabotage threats continue. (LRT, Anadolu Agency)
6. Lithuania's teachers began an open-ended strike on Monday, and thousands of them stayed away from work. They say the dispute isn't only about pay, but also about not being listened to. (LRT, Delfi)
7. The United States plans to tighten China's access to AI chips just as President Trump heads to Asia. He's due to meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in China, a meeting Beijing calls groundbreaking. (Reuters)
8. US envoy Jared Kushner says there's been progress in talks on Ukraine, and President Zelensky confirmed a new round of talks with American and European officials. Meanwhile, the IMF says it's close to a 1.6 billion dollar deal with Kyiv. (Reuters)
9. Italian prosecutors have placed Revolut under court administration over suspected money laundering. That's significant for Lithuania, since Revolut's European bank is licensed here. (Reuters, LRT)
10. From January, the cap on the regulated electricity price for households in Lithuania goes up by 23 percent. (LRT)

## Markets & Economy

- European stocks slipped on Monday, dragged down by tech and mining shares. UK stocks were steady ahead of jobs and inflation figures this week. (Reuters)
- Fed officials are focused on artificial intelligence. Chair Kevin Warsh says new technology will have a big impact but brings risks, and Michael Barr warned about AI risks to the financial system. Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack repeated her case for slow, steady rate hikes. (Reuters)
- Investors cooled on data-centre stocks in the third quarter, according to new filings. Berkshire Hathaway cut its stakes in Bank of America and Capital One and bought more Delta. (Reuters)
- Nvidia says Jensen Huang won't come back as CEO, even as a campaign for his return grows. Separately, the Trump administration is reportedly planning to name Nvidia, OpenAI and others as national AI champions. (Reuters)
- It was a busy day for deals. Johnson & Johnson is buying Ollin Biosciences for up to 3.3 billion dollars, Eli Lilly is buying Calytrix for up to 1.5 billion, and Canadian Natural Resources is buying Advantage Energy for 1.77 billion. (Reuters)
- Trump Media could face a one billion dollar debt repayment, with its stock close to record lows. (Reuters)
- Tesla says it will invest 3 billion euros to expand its factory in Germany. (LRT, 15min)
- Bank of Japan board member Takata is calling for a quick rate hike, and India's wholesale inflation jumped to over 12 percent in October because of fuel prices. (Reuters)
- Bank of Canada Governor Tiff Macklem will step down when his term ends in 2027. (Reuters)
- Supertanker freight rates have fallen more than 60 percent from their recent record highs. (Reuters)
