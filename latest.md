# News Digest — 16 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Asian stock markets opened the week lower. Investors are getting nervous ahead of Nvidia's earnings and Anthropic's share listing, and both will test how much life the tech rally has left. (Reuters)
2. Japan's economy unexpectedly shrank in the July to September quarter, by 0.1 percent on an annualised basis, because rising prices are squeezing households. The yen barely moved on the news. (Reuters)
3. The Gulf is getting more dangerous. Iran says it attacked a US base, and sirens and explosions were reported in Bahrain. Yemen's Houthis fired ballistic missiles toward Riyadh, and government forces in Yemen pulled back from a key strait. Iran also says a tanker carrying more than two million barrels of oil sank in the Persian Gulf. (Reuters, LRT, 15min)
4. In Lithuania, the education workers' union is starting an open-ended strike today and plans a rally outside parliament. The organisers say many more teachers signed up than they expected. (15min, Delfi, LRT)
5. Estonia says it stopped an attempt to set fire to a drone factory, and suspects Russian intelligence was behind it. It will close its border crossings with Russia from December. (Reuters, 15min, Delfi)
6. Oil prices are falling because traders expect a supply glut. Gold is edging up as markets wait for remarks from Fed chair Kevin Warsh for clues on interest rates. (Reuters)
7. The Nvidia leadership saga goes on. Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent says the company has to look outside for a new CEO, while the Financial Times reports that Jensen Huang has been talking to allies about a comeback. (Reuters, LRT, Delfi)
8. Lithuania's government wants to give the State Tax Inspectorate more powers, and business groups are worried that honest companies could get caught up in it. Meanwhile, budget talks are exposing rifts inside the ruling coalition. (LRT)
9. airBaltic says data on about 618 thousand passengers leaked from the systems of an outside IT supplier. (15min)
10. Hundreds of thousands of people rallied in Prague against the Czech government's plan to change how public media is funded. (Reuters, LRT, 15min, Delfi)

## Lithuania

- President Nausėda says no one will ever agree to Russia's demands about NATO's eastern flank. Lithuania is also urging the EU to label Russia's military intelligence, the GRU, and other Russian security services as terrorist organisations. (uatv.ua, Nasha Niva)
- Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys is in Brussels today. He'll talk with EU counterparts about support for Ukraine and how to respond to hybrid attacks. (15min)
- A new rotation of US troops arrives today at the Pabradė training ground. Separately, a big military exercise with helicopters and drones is starting in south-eastern Lithuania. (15min)
- The argument over Taiwan's representative office in Vilnius keeps splitting politicians. Former minister and current EU Commissioner Andrius Kubilius says it was China that cut diplomatic ties, so it's up to Beijing whether to restore them. (LRT, Delfi, 15min)
- A planned referendum on the family is drawing criticism. Equal opportunities ombudsperson Birutė Sabatauskaitė says its goals are unclear and it divides society. (LRT)
- New rules are coming for buying phones on instalments. Lenders will have to check both your income and your debts. (Delfi)
- Rents in Lithuania are rising faster than the EU average, and some new apartments are selling out before they're even built. (Delfi)
- Defence industry exports are growing remarkably fast, though experts warn the boom has risks. (Delfi)
- Fuel prices may come down, but experts say not to count on it. (Delfi)
- Lithuania has taken over the presidency of the Baltic Assembly from Estonia. (LSM)
- A British tourist who smashed a statue of Jesus in Vilnius isn't allowed to leave Lithuania for now while police investigate. (15min, GB News)
- Some State Tax Inspectorate e-services will be offline for a while today because of a systems upgrade. (15min)
