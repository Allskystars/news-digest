# News Digest — Thursday, 8 October 2026

## Top 10

1. Lithuania says it will deploy troops along its border with Russia's Kaliningrad region, after an incident at the Latvian border where armed, masked people tried to push migrants across. (LRT, Kyiv Independent)
2. Lithuanian lawmakers took another step toward lifting the country's ban on hosting nuclear weapons. The Kremlin warns this risks further escalation. (DW, IntelliNews)
3. President Trump says he will "certainly look at" a permanent US military base in Lithuania. Lithuanian defence officials are already gathering information on what it would take. (Euronews, LRT)
4. Bond markets are under heavy pressure. UK 30-year gilt yields hit a 28-year high, and European bank shares fell as yields surged. (Reuters)
5. Wall Street ended lower, off record highs, as Treasury yields climbed. The Fed minutes showed some officials want to prepare for market stress. (Reuters)
6. Oil is rising again on Middle East supply worries and shipping attacks. The IEA says another 100 million barrels of emergency reserves are still to be released. (Reuters)
7. Bitcoin slipped below 83,000 dollars as oil jumped on a report about an Iran strike plan. (CoinDesk)
8. Samsung flagged an 80 billion dollar quarterly profit, the highest ever for a tech company, thanks to the AI chip boom. (Reuters, BBC)
9. India raised its policy rate by a quarter point, its first hike in nearly four years. Indian shares opened lower. (Reuters)
10. The EU, Canada and Lithuania pulled out of a disinformation conference in Vilnius after pressure from US officials. (Politico, The Guardian)

## Lithuania

- Lithuania's interior minister says the army will help guard the border with Kaliningrad. This follows an incident on the Latvian side, where armed and masked people tried to break through and let migrants in. (LRT, 15min)
- Parliament moved forward on a constitutional change that would allow allied nuclear weapons on Lithuanian soil. Moscow has threatened "necessary measures," and Lithuania sent Russia a note of protest. (DW, IntelliNews, Inbox)
- Trump said he would look at a permanent US base in Lithuania. Vilnius reacted with excitement and a lot of questions, and the Defence Ministry is collecting information on conditions for stationing US troops. (Euronews, LRT)
- Lithuania received its first batch of American Switchblade 600 attack drones, as promised back in 2024. (AeroTime, Defence Industry Europe)
- The government extended temporary protection for Ukrainians until March 2028. Men who have not met their military obligations will be excluded from the automatic extension. (LRT)
- Lithuania's Health Minister says the risk of a plague outbreak spreading from Russia is very low, and that the country has enough antibiotics for a month. Lithuania could restrict transit from Russia if the threat is confirmed. (LRT, Delfi, Baltic Times)
- Conservative MP and former interior minister Agnė Bilotaitė is leaving parliament. (LRT, 15min)
- A cut cable in Vilnius disrupted several clinics, radio stations and some state offices. Service has since been restored. (15min, LRT, Delfi)
- A Russian man who swam to Lithuania seeking asylum was returned to Russia and sentenced to six years in prison. (Delfi, LRT, Meduza)
- Economy: Business groups warn that planned limits on foreign workers could hurt Lithuania's growth. (Anadolu, RTE)
- Economy: Parliament is debating fuel price caps, but fuel station representatives and a parliamentary committee are critical. One expert warned diesel could climb as high as three euros a litre. (LRT)
- Economy: Pensions are expected to rise from January, according to the prime minister. (Delfi, 15min)
- Economy: Lithuania has secured about half of the biofuel it needs for the heating season. (Delfi)
- Business: Lithuania and Poland will sign an agreement on the Harmony Link connection point. (15min)
- Business: The Baltic states are asking for ten billion euros to fund Rail Baltica. (Railway PRO)
- Business: Registry data show the real estate market grew by eight percent this year. (LRT)
- Business: Farmers will receive about 332 million euros in direct payments this month, and small and medium firms get 7.5 million euros for adopting AI. (LRT)
- Business: A new poll gives an early look at the favourites in the Vilnius mayoral race. (15min)

## Markets & Economy

- A global bond sell-off dominated the day. UK 30-year gilt yields reached their highest in 28 years, European bank shares fell as spreads widened, and the FTSE 100 fell with banks leading the decline. (Reuters)
- On Wall Street, stocks ended lower and off record highs as Treasury yields climbed. A strong ten-year auction later eased the bond sell-off. (Reuters)
- Fed minutes showed some officials want to prepare for market stress. The dollar edged back from an 18-month high afterward. (Reuters)
- US 30-year mortgage rates are at their highest in nearly three years. (Reuters)
- France is in focus. A bond sell-off leaves little room for fiscal mistakes, and sterling hit a 16-month high against the euro on French budget worries. The Bank of France says it needs no help from the ECB right now. (Reuters)
- Oil is rising on Middle East supply concerns. Shell expects record refining margins, and the IEA says 100 million barrels of reserves are still to be released. (Reuters)
- The IMF chief warned that an energy shock, growing debt and AI risks threaten global growth. (Reuters)
- India raised rates by a quarter point, and its currency reserves have fallen for a fourth week. (Reuters)
- Samsung's record 80 billion dollar profit highlights the AI boom, while Mercedes-Benz car sales fell eight percent on weak demand in China. (Reuters, BBC)
- In the UK, Boots was sold in a seven billion pound deal to a Canadian billionaire family, and Royal Mail plans to cut 2,500 jobs. (BBC)
- Germany blocked the sale of a logistics company to China's Cosco, while German industrial output rose more than expected in August. (Reuters)
- Gold recovered from a two-month low as the dollar rally stalled. (Reuters)
