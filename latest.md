# News Digest — 21 November 2026

## Top 10

1. The AI sell-off turned into a rout. The Nasdaq plunged 5 percent on Friday, the day after Anthropic's debut, and both the S&P 500 and the Dow are now officially in correction territory. (Reuters)
2. Anthropic's shares dived about 13 percent on their second day of trading, leaving many retail buyers with losses, after the IPO bank closed a position that had been propping up the stock. The listing itself was a record: 81 billion dollars raised at a 1.5 trillion dollar valuation, rising to 93.5 billion once the extra shares were sold. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
3. Russian missiles and a drone came down and exploded in Poland. Warsaw blames Moscow, NATO says it reacted immediately, and Lithuania's presidency says it would back Poland if it asks for NATO Article 4 consultations. (LRT, 15min)
4. Bitcoin fell below 59,000 dollars on its third straight day of losses, and is closing in on its summer low. BlackRock's bitcoin fund saw a record one-day outflow of 541 million dollars. (CoinDesk, Reuters)
5. Reuters reports, citing sources, that the US Securities and Exchange Commission is looking into Nvidia's accounting. Nvidia's falling share price has already pushed it below Alphabet in market value. (Reuters)
6. Wall Street's fear gauge, the VIX, hit a seven-month high, and European shares had their worst week in more than a year as the AI trade unravelled. (Reuters)
7. Fed governor Lisa Cook says interest rate hikes are needed to bring down high inflation, while Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent argued for a smaller hike, pointing to the energy shock. (Reuters)
8. Britain and the EU agreed a reset deal covering food trade and youth mobility, and they plan to link their carbon markets. (Reuters, LRT)
9. Gold fell to its lowest level since September last year, and analysts say the same pressure is weighing on bitcoin too. (CoinDesk)
10. Cybersecurity firm CrowdStrike is in talks to buy rival Zscaler, according to sources. (Reuters)

## Lithuania

- Lithuania's presidency says that if Warsaw asks to trigger NATO's Article 4 after the missile incident in Poland, Lithuania will support it. Lithuania also lodged a strong protest with a representative of the Russian embassy in Vilnius. (LRT, 15min)
- Germany's army, the Bundeswehr, has finished forming the brigade that is being stationed in Lithuania. NATO troops also practised a river crossing over the Nemunas in Germany's biggest-ever drills here. (LRT, 15min, TVP World)
- Lithuanian soldiers joined European exercises rehearsing a possible post-war mission in Ukraine. President Nausėda says Trump expects Europe to play a bigger role in Ukraine peace efforts. (LRT, Anadolu)
- Lithuanians who had been held for almost two months in the Democratic Republic of Congo on spying accusations have been freed and are heading home. (15min)
- A Seimas committee backed a plan to make it simpler to strip citizenship from people who serve in foreign armies, such as those of Russia or Belarus. (LRT)
- The China question keeps simmering. An opposition leader is threatening a no-confidence motion against Foreign Minister Budrys over the Taiwan office row, and the presidency says normalising ties with China isn't a topic for the State Defence Council. (Rti, Delfi)
- The teachers' strike goes on. The union says a joint rally with other organisations is planned for 1 December, and lawyer Egidijus Kūris has publicly sided with the strikers. (LRT, Delfi)
- MP Ignas Vėgėlė's new party, Citizens' Lithuania, will hold its founding congress in January. (Delfi, 15min)
- Lithuania has the fifth-lowest tax revenue in the EU as a share of GDP, according to new figures. (LRT)
- The Prime Minister says the government is focusing on investment in the defence industry, but finding enough workers is a challenge. (LRT)
- Energy group Ignitis has confirmed its new chief executive. The energy minister says any cut to electricity and gas tariffs is up to Ignitis and the state, and the heating season is likely to cost even more than forecast. (15min, LRT)
- Lithuania has designated carbon dioxide terminals as national energy infrastructure. (CEEnergynews)
- Clinical research firm Biomapas is buying Pinnaxis and part of Alira Health's business to expand in the US. Twenty-three Lithuanian firms made a ranking of top Central and Eastern European companies. (Investors in Healthcare, Delfi)
- Startup Kilo has paused five apps while journalists look into its business, and fellow startup Pulsetto temporarily halted sales after US customers complained of burns. (LRT, 15min, Delfi)
- A court ruled that Baltic Foods broke the law by supplying unsafe poultry. (LRT)
- From mid-December, rail operator LTG Link will run a second daily train to Riga, this time via Daugavpils in Latvia. (LRT, Delfi)
- Foreign Minister Budrys will visit Azerbaijan next week, ahead of Lithuania's EU presidency. (Caspian Post, Report.az)
- Weather: winter is creeping in, with sleet and black ice expected this weekend, so take care on the roads. (LRT, 15min)
