# News Digest, Friday 9 October 2026

## Top 10

1. Bitcoin bounced back to about eighty-two thousand dollars after President Trump said the US won't strike Iran before the November midterms, and oil prices dropped. That's from CoinDesk and Reuters.
2. A big crypto sell-off wiped out about a billion dollars in leveraged bets, and traders betting on ether got hit about six times harder than those betting on bitcoin. CoinDesk.
3. Lithuania is sending up to a hundred soldiers to help guard its border with Russia's Kaliningrad region, after a border breach between Belarus and Latvia and worries about a possible Russian mobilization. President Nausėda calls it insurance against provocations. LRT, Euromaidan Press and Bloomberg.
4. Lithuania's prime minister says there will be no cut to fuel excise tax, because it would blow another hole in the budget. The ruling coalition's council met and adopted no new measures to lower fuel prices. LRT and Delfi.
5. Lithuania's 2027 budget puts more money into defence and pensions, and public sector pay is set to rise. Teachers, for example, are due a pay rise of about seven and a half percent. Delfi, LRT and 15min.
6. On Wall Street, the S&P 500 and Nasdaq ended lower on Thursday as crude prices jumped and chip stocks weighed on the market. Reuters.
7. Oil fell on Friday after Trump's comments on Iran talks eased supply worries, though attacks on tankers around the Strait of Hormuz remain a risk. Reuters.
8. Federal Reserve governor Waller said more rate hikes are needed, though there's flexibility on the pace. The European Central Bank is also expected to hike again in December. Reuters.
9. The White House has blocked Microsoft from a foreign-worker hiring programme, with green cards for IT firms frozen. BBC and Reuters.
10. Lithuania says it could pay for a permanent US military base in the country. Breaking Defense.

- The Nobel Prize in Literature went to Canadian writer Anne Carson. LRT, 15min and Delfi.
