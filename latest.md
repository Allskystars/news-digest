# News Digest — 20 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Anthropic made its Nasdaq debut. Priced at 83 dollars, the shares opened at 111 dollars 50, slipped back under 100, and still finished well above the offering price, putting the company's value somewhere between one and a half and two trillion dollars. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
2. The debut didn't lift the wider market. The Nasdaq, the S&P 500 and the Dow each fell more than 2 percent on Thursday, and Asian shares are heading for their worst week since 2020 as the AI sell-off deepens. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
3. Bitcoin has dropped to a four-month low, its weakest level since July, with more than 300 million dollars pulled out of bitcoin ETFs on Wednesday alone. (CoinDesk)
4. Expectations of higher US interest rates keep growing. J.P. Morgan now expects the Fed to hike at each of its next three meetings, and gold has slid to a 14-month low on a strong dollar. (Reuters)
5. Trump, Xi and Putin wrapped up their summit in China with what they call a "Peace Declaration". European leaders were unimpressed: Germany's Friedrich Merz called it a photo session, and Poland voiced alarm at Trump's tone towards Putin. (LRT, 15min, Delfi, Reuters)
6. Lithuania's Seimas has sent the 2027 state budget back to the government for revision after a heated first reading. Prime Minister Sinkevičius says a plan to cut the deficit will come in spring. (LRT, Delfi)
7. Yemen's Houthis say they targeted King Khalid airport and the financial district in Riyadh, damaging the city's tallest building, and oil prices edged higher on supply worries. (Reuters, 15min)
8. JPMorgan, Visa and Mastercard say some of their third-party vendors were hit in a cyberattack. Separately, Trump fired his national cyber director after a string of tech outages drew criticism. (Reuters)
9. Citigroup understated its risky assets by about 35 billion dollars after recording trades incorrectly, according to a Reuters exclusive. (Reuters)
10. Lithuania has a plan to fortify its border with Russia's Kaliningrad region with physical barriers, and President Nausėda insists the country can still build a full army division by 2030. (LRT)

## Lithuania

- The Seimas also sent the 2027 Sodra social insurance budget back to the government. Healthcare workers picketed outside parliament over pay, and the health minister admitted the planned raise for public health specialists isn't enough. (LRT, 15min)
- Lithuania plans to cut its quota for foreign workers to about 24,400 in 2027. (LRT, 15min)
- The Liberals are threatening a no-confidence motion against Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys if he pushes to rename the Taiwanese Representative Office in Vilnius. (LRT)
- The State Audit Office says Lithuania needs higher defence spending to hit its military goals. The presidency says building the division can be matched to what the budget allows. (LRT)
- Lithuania's military aid to Ukraine won't shrink next year, the defence minister says. Former foreign minister Gabrielius Landsbergis said Trump looked more like Putin's ally than the West's representative at the summit. (Ukrinform, LRT)
- Bank of Lithuania governor Gediminas Šimkus says fraud has become an organised system, and he accused the Finance Ministry of dragging its feet on anti-fraud amendments. (Delfi, LRT)
- Prime Minister Sinkevičius says cutting red tape for business is one of the government's top priorities, while debate continues over plagiarism claims about his doctoral thesis. (Delfi, LRT)
- A 450 million euro electrified rail section between Vilnius and Klaipėda has opened. (LRT)
- Artea Bank and the European Investment Bank are backing a 625 million euro fund to renovate around 25,000 Lithuanian homes. (Dealroom)
- Estonia's Infortar is buying the agri-business Litagra from Akola Group for 63 million euros, and Latvia's Amber Latvijas balzams is moving some production to Lithuania. (Dealroom, LRT, LSM)
- Politician Savickas warned that even discussing a return of Russian gas to Europe would be a strategic mistake. Poland is also deepening regional gas cooperation with Lithuania and three central European neighbours. (LRT, Notes From Poland)
- A Lithuanian border guard was sentenced for killing a cigarette smuggler, and a court found there was no ground to use a weapon. (LRT)
- Striking teachers are still out of the classroom, and the question of whether the strike is legal is getting sharper. The Seimas will decide in December on better conditions for platform workers. (15min, LRT)
