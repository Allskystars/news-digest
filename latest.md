# News Digest — Wednesday, 4 November 2026

## Top 10

1. The big story is the US midterm elections. Early results show Democrats winning ground, and projections say they'll take control of the House. Per 15min and Reuters, they also pulled off a surprise Senate win in South Carolina.
2. Markets reacted right away. Reuters says stocks and the dollar slid after the first Democratic wins, while betting markets increasingly priced in a Democratic sweep.
3. Oil dropped about seven percent after Iran signaled it's open to peace talks, according to Reuters.
4. Lithuania marked the hundredth birthday of former president Valdas Adamkus. There was a gala at the Opera and Ballet Theatre, with Delfi, LRT and 15min all covering it. Adamkus said he never could have achieved anything without the Lithuanian people.
5. Crypto took a huge hit. Binance lost about 6.3 billion dollars in a hack that CoinDesk and the BBC say began with fake job interviews. The exchange has seen roughly 5.3 billion dollars in net outflows, and its co-CEO told Reuters the company is in very strong financial health.
6. Lithuania's parliament has taken a first step toward removing the constitutional ban on nuclear weapons and foreign bases, though the story so far comes mainly from smaller outlets.
7. Russia and Ukraine are set to hold talks in Abu Dhabi next week. The Kremlin confirmed this, per Reuters, even as Russia pounded Ukraine's power grid.
8. Latvia declared an air alert overnight after a drone fell on its territory, 15min reports. Poland, meanwhile, has deployed counterterrorism police near the Kaliningrad border, per LRT.
9. AMD gave a strong forecast that eased fears about AI spending, with data-center sales surging, according to Reuters. Meta also raised 15 billion dollars in a bond sale.
10. Australia's central bank raised interest rates to an 18-year high, though Reuters says a split vote hints at a pause ahead.

## Lithuania

- The Lithuanian president questioned the term "hybrid threats" for Russian operations, saying some of the attacks are plainly kinetic. LRT and Anadolu both reported it.
- Lithuania is also backing sanctions on Belarusian fertiliser. Per LRT, officials say a Politico report about US pressure to reopen the port to Belarusian potash is outdated.
- Lithuania has tightened the rules for foreigners applying for temporary residence permits, LRT reports.
- The government is weighing a state-owned drone industry holding company, according to 15min. A Seimas committee is also looking at temporarily cutting intercity bus ticket prices.
- The government has also ordered Lithuanian Post to keep providing the universal postal service for nine more years, 15min says.
- On the economy, LRT reports that Klaipėda port's infrastructure fees will change next year. Heating compensation can also reach more than just the lowest-income households, so people are being told to check if they qualify. LRT and Delfi both covered that.
- Lithuania revoked the refugee status of a Belarusian businessman who had clashed with opposition leader Tsikhanouskaya, per Belsat.
- Border guards stopped 26 migrants at the Belarus border in recent days, Delfi reports. NATO jets also flew over Vilnius, which Delfi and 15min covered.
- Prime Minister Tusk said Poland received worrying information about activity near the Russian border, per Delfi.
- Airline airBaltic is scrapping its Vilnius to Düsseldorf route, LRT says. A string of bomb threats also forced emergency landings of six planes in Europe, per LRT.

## Markets & Economy

- European stocks slipped on Middle East uncertainty, and gold edged lower as the dollar and yields strengthened, per Reuters. The Nasdaq ended higher on Tuesday as AI heavyweights rebounded.
- US job openings jumped in September and layoffs hit their lowest in a year and a half, Reuters reports.
- China's services growth slowed to a three-month low. Reuters adds that the FT says Chinese regulators told banks to pause IPOs.
- BMW cut its automotive outlook for the second time, and Fresenius Medical cut its profit forecast, per Reuters. Aramco's third-quarter profit rose by a third despite attacks.
- Marathon agreed to sell two refineries and its marketing business to CVR Energy for 4.2 billion dollars, according to Reuters.
- Reuters reports Britain will make lifetime retirement income the default for private pensions. France will raise the cap on its Livret A savings account in February.
- From the BBC: design firm Canva announced major job cuts after what it called its hardest year yet. A Poundland supplier says it will lose millions if the retailer collapses.
- Also from the BBC: drivers may get compensation offers before the car finance saga ends, and economists are asking whether "quiet quitting" is hitting the economy.
- Reuters has a warning too: global markets were already fragile, and big AI bets could break them.

## Crypto

- Bitcoin steadied after dipping to about 76,300 dollars, with ETF outflows and the midterms weighing on prices, CoinDesk says. It also notes bitcoin is breaking a midterm-election pattern that goes back to 2014.
- On the Binance hack, CoinDesk says it has burned through about half of its SAFU emergency fund, roughly 580 million dollars in bitcoin. The FBI pointed to North Korea's TraderTraitor group, and North Korea denies it.
- South Korea's president says the country needs a cybersecurity czar after the hack, and regulators asked the biggest banks how a major crypto hack would hit them.
- US regulators cleared perpetual futures on stocks, bringing the crypto trading model to Wall Street. Reuters adds that Cboe and Citadel Securities-backed groups are suing over it.
- Tether holds 150,000 bitcoin, worth about 11.5 billion dollars, its CEO says. Justin Sun is seeking sanctions against the Trump-linked World Liberty Financial.
- Smaller hits: Liquid's bitcoin IOUs are fully backed again after a 320 million dollar exploit, and Mercado Bitcoin pledged to cover user losses after a hack of nearly 40 million dollars.
