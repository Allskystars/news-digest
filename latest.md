# News Digest — 22 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Wall Street just had a brutal week. Reuters says the Nasdaq had its worst week since 2020, and both the Dow and the S&P 500 are now in correction territory. (Reuters)
2. Bitcoin had a wild Saturday. It dropped below 57,000 dollars, its lowest level in two years, and then bounced back above 60,000. About 1.8 billion dollars of leveraged crypto bets were wiped out along the way. (CoinDesk, Reuters)
3. Drones hit Russia's Kaliningrad region for the first time in the war, according to Ukrainian media. Sirens sounded and explosions were reported near the Baltic Fleet base in Baltiysk. Lithuania's crisis centre says it detected no drones, and that Lithuanian airspace is not being used for attacks. (LRT, Delfi, 15min, Kyiv Independent)
4. Seven Lithuanians who had been held in the Democratic Republic of Congo on suspicion of spying have been freed and are back home. Officials say they were simply tourists, detained over photos they took. (LRT)
5. A Russian cargo ship caught fire in the Gulf of Finland after what Moscow says was a drone strike, and its crew was evacuated. A Finnish official says the cargo may include military equipment. (Reuters, Delfi, 15min)
6. The EU and Indonesia signed a trade deal after ten years of negotiations. (Reuters, LRT, 15min)
7. The chair of EU leaders has proposed new cuts to the EU's 2028 to 2034 budget and suggested a digital tax on big tech. France's EU presidency called the proposal "very disappointing". (Reuters, 15min)
8. NATO chief Mark Rutte says the alliance will defend every inch of Polish territory, following the Russian missile incident in Poland. (Reuters, 15min)
9. The COP31 climate talks ran deep into overtime because of disputes over fossil fuels, but the summit president now says a final deal is close. (Reuters, LRT)
10. A new poll shows the Conservatives and the Social Democrats leading Lithuania's party ratings. (LRT)

## Lithuania

- President Nausėda says the investigation into Lithuanian citizens detained in Iran could take a while, and that it will need patience. (15min, LRT)
- Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys said of the Lithuanians freed in Congo that no one would be left behind. Prime Minister Sinkevičius added that they were never left in the dark. Budrys also argued that if the EU wants to survive, it has to become a global player. (LRT, 15min)
- Voting is under way today in a by-election for a Seimas seat in the Nalšia northern constituency, so a ban on campaigning is now in force. (LRT, 15min)
- Several ministries want to cut state-funded nursing care in hospitals to 90 days, and NGOs are asking what will happen to people who can't pay. (LRT)
- A proposal would let Lithuanian politicians be members of European political parties. (LRT)
- The Interior Minister says about half of the migrants due under the EU's solidarity mechanism have now been moved to Lithuania. Meanwhile, 14 irregular migrants were turned back at the Belarus border. (LRT, 15min)
- Kaliningrad issued an alert over what it called a suspicious object coming from Lithuania. Delfi also looks at the Rūdninkai training ground, where the German brigade will be based, and at US rotational forces training in Lithuania. (Apa.az, Delfi)
- Lithuania is planning a 36 percent tax on online gambling, according to an industry roundup. (European Gaming)
- On the business side, discount retailer Marytė Retail opened its seventh store. A court ruled that Klaipėda does not have to return 0.6 million euros of support for courtyard upgrades. And 11 million euros has been set aside for culture projects, including the renovation of Kaunas Central Post Office. (Delfi, 15min, LRT)
- The social security ministry says more than 600 people with debts to bailiffs have used the new "debt holiday". Separately, Delfi explains what is changing in how company shares will be managed. (Delfi)
- Customs officers found 10,000 packs of smuggled cigarettes in a garage in Tauragė, and in Vilnius a man was killed after running into the road in front of a BMW. (LRT, Delfi, 15min)

## Crypto

- Bitcoin fell below 57,000 dollars for the first time in two years, then rebounded about 9 percent to above 60,000. The selloff wiped out 1.8 billion dollars of leveraged positions, mostly bets that prices would go up. (CoinDesk, Reuters)
- XRP fell 10 percent. Long bets unwound, and assets in XRP ETFs slipped below one billion dollars. (CoinDesk)
- Ether treasury company BTCS warned the SEC that its 54 million dollar loan on Aave could be liquidated if ether prices keep falling. (CoinDesk)
- The FLIP token of cross-chain protocol Chainflip dropped 30 percent after a suspected 4.86 million dollar drain from one of its vaults. (CoinDesk)
- Bitcoin Core shipped fixes for a bug that could stall nodes by repeating a single ID 50,000 times. An AI-powered code scanner from Block's Spiral team found it. (CoinDesk)
