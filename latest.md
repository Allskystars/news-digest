# News Digest — 25 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Monday's Nvidia-fuelled rally is fading. Stocks slipped in the US, Europe and Asia as the AI rebound stumbled and new tariff threats came back into view. (Reuters)
2. The Trump administration is set to unveil new tariffs after a probe into excess factory capacity, possibly as soon as this week. China, Japan and South Korea could be among the targets. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
3. The Nvidia saga keeps going. A source says Jensen Huang admitted he violated the company's code of conduct, Senator Schumer accuses him of breaking export control law, and President Trump says he'd be willing to pardon him. (Reuters, CoinDesk)
4. Bitcoin has slipped back below 60,000 dollars, giving up all of Monday's gains. Investors pulled a record 4.7 billion dollars from US spot bitcoin funds in November. (CoinDesk)
5. Anthropic shares fell below their 83-dollar IPO price, putting the company on track to lose nearly 200 billion dollars in market value. (Reuters)
6. US consumer confidence jumped to its highest level in nearly two years, a rare piece of upbeat news for the economy. (Reuters)
7. A journalists' investigation found Lithuanian prepaid SIM cards inside Russian drones used to attack Ukraine. The European Commission is calling on member states to look into it, and the president's office has responded. (LRT, Delfi, 15min)
8. Lithuania's teachers are still on strike. Union leader Andrius Navickas says talks at the government didn't settle everything, and the prime minister says bigger pay rises are a question for the future because this year's money is already spent. (LRT)
9. The EU has set limits on imports of hybrid cars from China and other countries. (Reuters, Delfi)
10. China Southern Airlines has ordered 50 jets from Boeing, the company's biggest deal in China in nearly a decade. (Reuters)

## Lithuania

- The European Court of Human Rights ruled on migrants pushed back to Belarus by Poland, Latvia and Lithuania. LRT reports that Lithuania itself was found not to have violated human rights, while the court found violations in the overall set of cases. (LRT, Notes From Poland)
- German tanks have arrived in Lithuania by sea for exercises, largely unnoticed, which denied Moscow a propaganda opportunity. The German army is also testing kamikaze drones and an AI targeting tool there. (LRT, Baltic News Network)
- The conservative leader is urging NATO to start targeting Russian shadow-fleet oil tankers in the Baltic Sea. (LRT)
- A presidential adviser says the warnings in Kaliningrad reflect Russian fears, not an escalation. (LRT)
- The defence minister has sharply attacked his coalition partners, accusing them of political populism. (15min)
- A new poll of how people rate ministers puts Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys at the top, while the prime minister's right-hand man is at the very bottom. Budrys is heading to Baku for a business forum with Azerbaijan. (LRT, Trend)
- The president's adviser says the question of reviewing pensions is closed, and that taxes in Lithuania must not be designed around a single company. (Delfi)
- President Nausėda will present the priorities of Lithuania's upcoming EU Council presidency to Dutch leaders. Meanwhile, negotiations on the EU's long-term budget are nearing their climax, with a lot at stake for Lithuania. (15min, LRT)
- The Bank of Lithuania says house price growth is slowing and the market is getting a bit calmer. (LRT)
- Lithuania plans to renovate 519,000 buildings by 2050, at a cost of about 66 billion euros. (Delfi)
- Drinks makers are warning about what a steep rise in the sugar tax could lead to. (Delfi)
- Paper maker Grigeo saw its nine-month EBITDA fall 9 percent on lower profitability. (Delfi, TradingView)
- Construction group Merko will build a new residential quarter called Brastos on the Neris riverbank in Kaunas. (15min, Yahoo Finance)
- Lithuania has given Ukraine more than 1 billion dollars in military aid since 2022, according to the General Staff. (Ukrinform)
- A presidential aide says Taiwan should change the Chinese name of its Vilnius office. (LRT)
- The appeals court is set to rule in the expenses case of MP Andrius Bagdonas. And LRT's director general, Monika Garbačiauskaitė-Budrienė, has filed a complaint over her salary. (15min)
- A Rimtas Kalpokas painting, missing since 1939, has been found dumped next to rubbish bins in Vilnius. Museum staff call it an absolute sensation. (Delfi, LRT)
- Lithuania has recorded its first African swine fever outbreak in pigs this year. (Feed Strategy)
