# News Digest — Saturday, October 24, 2026

## Top 10

1. Oil is the big story. Brent crude jumped above 116 dollars a barrel, the highest in six months, according to CoinDesk's market coverage. Reuters adds that the US has authorised an emergency release of up to 400,000 barrels from its strategic reserve.
2. Wall Street closed lower to end a rocky week, says Reuters. Hedge fund returns also got worse as the tech selloff deepened, though trading in AI shares was quieter on Friday after a slump the day before.
3. France is under strain. Reuters reports European stocks posted a weekly loss on French turmoil and high oil prices, and Moody's spared France a ratings downgrade. Meanwhile 15min and LRT say Paris is bracing for a new wave of protests, with around seventy thousand police to be deployed.
4. Russia and Ukraine have agreed a ceasefire in the embattled town of Oleshky, and will hold peace talks next month, Reuters reports. The Kremlin confirmed the ceasefire.
5. Fitch has affirmed Lithuania's credit rating at A-plus with a stable outlook, saying growth offsets the risks. The news came from Fitch, via Yahoo Finance and TradingView.
6. Euro zone growth slowed to a four-month low in October, Reuters says, with rising costs and French unrest weighing on business activity. German growth also softened.
7. Bitcoin is holding above 81 thousand dollars, according to CoinDesk, even as oil prices climb.
8. Lithuanian authorities detained six people over cigarette and balloon smuggling from Belarus, Delfi and LRT report, and another border guard officer was detained in the same wider case.
9. India's Reliance Jio IPO has become the most subscribed in the country's history, with about 77 billion dollars in bids, says Reuters.
10. Reuters reports that Shein has agreed to pay 100 million dollars to settle a US Federal Trade Commission probe.

## Lithuania

- Lithuania's environment agency has approved plans for a Rheinmetall ammunition factory, according to LRT. Separately, the Giraitė arms factory showed off its first assembled automatic rifles, as NATO-standard weapons begin to be produced in Lithuania.
- The Finance Minister defended the deficit budget, saying there was pressure to cut defence spending, reports LRT. Lithuanian firms are also making anti-drone netting to protect strategic sites.
- Prosecutors say twelve people are charged in a suspected 1.3 million euro fraud involving EU-funded projects, after more than a hundred investigators carried out twenty-five searches, according to the EU prosecutor's office, Delfi and 15min.
- Vilnius Mayor Valdas Benkunskas says he will run for a second term, LRT and Delfi report.
- Ignitis is asking to postpone Lithuania's three-billion-dollar offshore wind project, says Splash247. Its subsidiary is also considering a gas-fired power plant in Estonia, according to ERR.
- LRT says Lithuania is considering bringing back checks on the Latvian border, and Lithuanians are driving to Latvia for cheaper food and fuel.
- A new Vilnius bus station design has been unveiled, says Delfi. Lithuania's airports will offer seventy-four routes this winter, six fewer than last year, according to LRT.
- Delfi and LRT report that two Lithuanian women who went missing in Iran are being detained, according to a guide who spoke to a traveller.
- In Kaunas, a ten-car pile-up on the ring road injured a police officer on her way to work, Delfi reports.

## Markets & Economy

- Sterling hit a fresh seventeen-month high against the euro after UK data, says Reuters. UK businesses reported their strongest month since February, but retail sales fell as consumer confidence dropped.
- A Reuters poll expects the Bank of Canada to raise rates to 2.5 percent on October 28, and once more by the end of March.
- Reuters says the White House is easing tariffs on some Canadian goods from October 30, a move reported by the BBC as well, as the midterm elections loom.
- A tanker carrying about forty thousand tonnes of Russian diesel is heading to the US, according to LRT, citing a Putin–Trump deal also reported by Reuters.
- Global equity funds attracted inflows for a fifth week in a row, Reuters says. Reuters also reports Meta is creating a new class of non-voting shares.
- Italy's central bank is calling for banks to invest in digital assets despite uncertainty, says Reuters. Citi won a licence for a fully owned securities unit in China.
- The BBC reports that California voters will soon decide on a first-of-its-kind tax on billionaires.

## Crypto

- Bitcoin steadied near 81,700 dollars, with altcoins rebounding and the memecoin Pepe up thirteen percent, says CoinDesk. It also notes bitcoin has been about as jumpy as Nvidia.
- Robinhood says bitcoin, gold and oil prediction markets are among its fastest growing, according to CoinDesk.
- HSBC is rolling out its first stablecoin to more than three million app users in Hong Kong, reports CoinDesk.
- Bitcoin Core developers changed their security disclosure rules because AI makes finding bugs so easy, says CoinDesk. Ethereum also delayed a test of its Glamsterdam upgrade after developers flagged a risk to existing contracts.
- India's CoinDCX denies it was hacked after investigators flagged about 16 million dollars in suspicious outflows, according to CoinDesk. Polymarket has reportedly fired its marketing chief, also per CoinDesk.
- Argentina has dropped its investigation into President Milei over a crypto fraud allegation, says the BBC.
