# News Digest — Tuesday, 27 October 2026

## Top 10

1. Anthropic has filed for a blockbuster stock market listing, and it lifted AI shares around the world. Reuters says Asian stocks surged and US equities ended higher, though investors are still cautious about the huge losses behind the sales growth.
2. The company also warned of a "catastrophic risk" to humankind in its filing, according to the BBC. Reuters notes its soaring sales come with steep losses, so the IPO is seen as a real test for the AI boom.
3. Central banks are the big theme this week. The Fed, the European Central Bank and the Bank of Japan all announce rate decisions, and Reuters expects the Fed to pause its hikes, though maybe not for long.
4. Brazilian markets fell hard after Lula's election win. Reuters says stocks and the real tumbled, while Petrobras's boss called the selloff speculative and sees a buying opportunity.
5. Bitcoin is hovering right around 80,000 dollars. CoinDesk says traders are de-risking ahead of the Fed meeting, even as oil prices retreat.
6. Meta is reportedly looking at a record 95 billion dollar equity raise to pay for its AI expansion, according to Reuters.
7. In Lithuania, Fitch has affirmed the country's A-plus credit rating with a stable outlook, according to LRT.
8. Lithuania and South Korea are working together on air defence. LRT reports a South Korean firm signed deals with four Lithuanian companies, and another outlet says they agreed on joint production.
9. Lithuania is expanding the Šiauliai air base so it can host up to 750 NATO troops on rotation, LRT reports.
10. In the UK, the Budget is due Wednesday. Reuters says Chancellor Healey is expected to be careful with the markets, and sterling slipped against the euro and the dollar ahead of it.

## Lithuania

- Fitch has affirmed Lithuania's A-plus rating with a stable outlook. That's a vote of confidence in the economy. LRT.
- The Šiauliai air base will be expanded to take 500 to 750 NATO troops on a rotating basis, according to the defence ministry as reported by LRT and Lrytas.
- A South Korean company signed air defence deals with four Lithuanian firms. LRT. Separately, local defence companies are asking for more state help amid suspected sabotage and threats. LRT.
- The 2027 budget draft is under fire. LRT reports opposition figures say spending is rising on everything and fiscal discipline is being ignored, and a commentary calls the finance minister the weakest link in the government's first hundred days.
- A row over the head of customs continues. LRT says the candidate who was passed over, Matulionis, is now in dispute with the Finance Ministry, and the anti-corruption agency has got involved. 15min reports the winning candidate had links reaching to the prime minister's circle.
- Lithuania expects an EU vote in June 2027 on ending seasonal clock changes. LRT and Anadolu.
- Ukraine will be a priority in Lithuania's presidency of the EU Council in the first half of 2027, said Ukraine's parliament speaker Stefanchuk, according to Interfax-Ukraine. Zelensky also met Seimas speaker Juozas Olekas, per Ukrinform.
- On the border, Delfi says 25 migrants tried to cross from Belarus in the last three days, and LRT says 21 were turned back in the past day. Lithuania has also called on the EU to speed up checks on the origin of Russian grain.
- Hospitals are in the news. Delfi and LRT report Klaipėda University Hospital will not use the new pay model and is asking to freeze salaries for a year, while staff speak of pressure and debts.
- Next year Lithuania will issue a new three-euro collector coin, reported by Delfi and 15min.
- Delfi and 15min say Lithuania's highest average pay in September was 74,000 euros a month in one sector, per 15min. Lithuania has also taken over the chair of the Baltic Assembly, per LRT.
- Sad news for Lithuania's 100-year-old former president: 15min reports on Valdas Adamkus's health ahead of his hundredth birthday.

## Markets & Economy

- Global stocks rose on the Anthropic IPO filing, with Reuters reporting an Asian surge and a higher close in the US. European shares also rebounded on corporate optimism, per Reuters.
- Gold rose as lower oil prices eased inflation fears ahead of the Fed meeting. Reuters.
- The Bank of Canada is expected to hike rates on Wednesday to curb energy-driven inflation. Reuters.
- German business sentiment rose for the sixth month in a row, says the Ifo institute, and euro zone firms are finding it harder to get credit, per the ECB survey. Reuters.
- South Korea's third-quarter GDP beat forecasts as the AI chip boom lifted exports. Reuters.
- Big deals: EQT will buy Range Resources for 13 billion dollars to create a top US gas producer. DoorDash is reportedly in talks to buy Instacart for about 17 billion. Reuters.
- Microsoft is offering buyouts to around 60,000 employees to contain costs. Reuters. Skydance is also offering buyouts after the Warner Bros merger.
- Verizon raised its yearly forecast and signed an AI infrastructure deal worth more than 2 billion dollars with AWS. Reuters.
- Trend-following hedge funds had their worst week since 2024 as AI stocks fell, says Goldman, per Reuters.
- In the UK, the minimum wage is reported to rise nearly 4 percent, the Times says via Reuters. A retail body warns the "dam is starting to break" on price rises. And the BBC says Healey is promising a package of "everyday fixes" before the Budget.
- Apple says it will replace iPhone Duo handsets that have screen problems. BBC.

- The NBA has joined other sports leagues in working with the US commodities regulator on prediction markets, while the NFL holds back. CoinDesk and Reuters.
