# News Digest — 14 November 2026

## Top 10

1. The Nvidia fallout keeps spreading. Reuters reports that former chief executive Jensen Huang misled the company's top lawyer about his affair, Nvidia has approached Arm's boss Rene Haas about the top job, and some employees want Huang back. (Reuters)
2. The scandal has turned political in Washington. Treasury Secretary Bessent blamed the Commerce Department for not putting Nvidia partner Megaspeed under export controls sooner, and pressure is building on Commerce Secretary Lutnick. (Reuters, BBC)
3. Bitcoin dropped to about 68 thousand dollars, its lowest since August, and is heading for a sixth straight weekly loss. Analysts point to money moving toward Anthropic's upcoming IPO, a stronger dollar and the chance of Fed rate hikes. (CoinDesk)
4. The Fed is sounding more hawkish. San Francisco Fed president Mary Daly said the Fed will likely need to raise rates as inflation gets worse, and US producer prices rose again in October. (Reuters)
5. Lithuania had the steepest drop in economic output in the EU in the third quarter, out of the 20 countries that have reported so far. (LRT, 15min)
6. Poland has warned Lithuania not to put a windfall tax on Orlen's oil refinery. (LRT, Delfi)
7. European shares ended their best week since June on strong company earnings. Analysts say Europe is benefiting as investors move away from troubled US tech. (Reuters)
8. President Trump will meet Xi Jinping and Vladimir Putin in Shenzhen on Thursday, and nuclear arms cuts are on the agenda. Separately, the US and China held their first Board of Trade meeting. (Reuters)
9. Microsoft apologised for its global outage and says services are mostly back. Experts say it shows how much the world depends on a handful of big tech companies. (BBC)
10. Lithuania's teachers' union is planning what it calls its biggest strike yet, starting Monday. About 150 schools and 4 thousand teachers are expected to take part. (15min, Delfi)

## Lithuania

- Media reports say Russia wants Finland to leave NATO and wants a ban on bases in the Baltic states. Finland rejected that sharply, and Latvia's prime minister said Russia can't make security demands of Europe. (LRT, 15min, Delfi)
- Foreign Minister Budrys says Lithuania is "not in a trap" over its relations with China and Taiwan. Chinese diplomats recently visited Vilnius in a first step toward restoring ties, and youth groups have protested a plan to rename the Taiwanese office. (Delfi, LRT, Taiwan News)
- The prime minister downplayed the question of who should speak for Europe in Ukraine peace talks, saying a united position matters most. The foreign minister wants EU institutions at the table, but EU leaders agreed that the five biggest countries plus the EU will represent Europe. (15min, LRT, Delfi)
- Lithuania's academic ethics watchdog has opened a new investigation into the prime minister's doctoral thesis. (LRT)
- The army gave its explanation for why an air-defence missile missed a drone, and LRT asks whether expensive missiles make sense against cheap drones. (15min, LRT)
- Central bank chief Gediminas Šimkus spoke about the biggest threat to the economy and said the government should scrap tax breaks that make no economic sense. (Delfi, 15min)
- Winter energy bills are expected to rise sharply, possibly above 2023 levels. Electricity tariffs could go up by about 20 percent. (LRT, Baltic News Network)
- Grid operator Litgrid connected three more battery storage parks in October with a combined 168 megawatts, and construction has started on a 330-megawatt solar park, which would be Lithuania's largest. (LRT, PV Tech)
- State energy group EPSO-G reported higher profit and investment for the first nine months of the year. (Delfi, GlobeNewswire)
- Parties are lining up for next year's local elections. The Social Democrats are approving their programme and mayoral candidates, the Freedom Party is running candidates in ten municipalities, and the Greens have named their candidate for mayor of Vilnius. (15min, Delfi)
- Lithuanian Customs says a cyber incident may have exposed personal data. A man was also detained in Vilnius for tearing a Ukrainian flag down from the Transport Ministry building. (Lithuanian Government, LRT)
