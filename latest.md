# News Digest — 13 November 2026

## Top 10

1. Nvidia's Jensen Huang has quit as chief executive, effective immediately, after the relationship scandal. Chief financial officer Colette Kress is interim CEO, and US lawmakers want to question Huang over claims that one of the relationships was with a Chinese spy. (Reuters, BBC)
2. The shares slumped on the news but clawed back some losses later, helped by retail buyers. Overnight, stocks steadied while the dollar climbed with US bond yields. (Reuters)
3. The dollar is near a 19-month high as markets bet the Fed will raise rates, and the Japanese yen is testing 160 to the dollar. Fed officials Daly and Hammack both talked about a gradual path of rate hikes. (Reuters)
4. Bitcoin fell below 70 thousand dollars early Friday and ether dropped under 2 thousand, setting off big liquidations in futures. Outflows from US bitcoin funds have now run for five days. (CoinDesk)
5. Lithuania's coalition partners are calling the 2027 budget talks chaotic and are attacking Finance Minister Valys. President Nausėda says he could veto the budget if pension indexation is cut. (LRT, Delfi)
6. China sent diplomats to Vilnius in its first attempt to repair relations with Lithuania. Foreign Minister Kęstutis Budrys played it down, saying it was neither the most important meeting nor the last, and that no concrete decisions are coming yet. (Bloomberg, LRT, 15min)
7. Anthropic's share offering is reported to be about six times oversubscribed, and talks on a SpaceX–Tesla merger are said to be moving forward. (Reuters, citing Bloomberg)
8. US home sales fell to their lowest level in more than 16 years in October. The government also started its new fiscal year with a record October deficit of 404 billion dollars. (Reuters)
9. The UK economy grew 0.7 percent in the three months to September, its fastest pace in over two years. European shares hit a record high on strong earnings and cheaper energy. (BBC, Reuters)
10. Microsoft spent hours fixing a global outage that took down Outlook and Teams for thousands of users. (Reuters, BBC)

## Lithuania

- Lithuania plans a windfall tax that would hit the Polish-owned refinery Orlen Lietuva. The industrialists' confederation warns that the proposed excise and windfall tax changes could hurt the economy, while some politicians say a windfall tax should only be temporary. (TVP World, LRT, Delfi)
- The National Audit Office warns that without action, Lithuania's public debt could pass 74 percent of GDP by 2035. Politician Bartkus says he had warned that the money would run out. (LRT, Delfi)
- Electricity and gas bills are going up. People are also getting their first heating bills of the season. (Delfi, 15min, LRT)
- The prime minister does not support raising VAT on medicines, saying it would hit too many people. (LRT)
- Teachers are holding firm on a strike over pay, even though Education Minister Popovienė has asked them not to walk out. (15min, LRT)
- Luminor bank's profit is down 20 percent this year, to 108 million euros. (LRT)
- A survey found that one company in ten is increasing investment, while one in five is putting it off. (LRT)
- In energy, Lithuania is planning offshore "energy islands" and will need new flexible power plants. The ministry wants 2.8 times more battery storage by 2030, and the port operator KN Energies says a carbon dioxide terminal worth up to 300 million euros is technically possible. (Renewables Now, LRT)
- DS1 will build a 330-megawatt solar park in Lithuania for Israel's Energix. (Renewables Now)
- Vilnius biotech start-up Biomatter raised 18 million dollars to design new enzymes. (Dealroom)
- Lithuania is thinking about bringing back checks on the border with Latvia. Police also broke up another cigarette-smuggling ring that used weather balloons, though balloon smuggling from Belarus has dropped by two thirds. (LSM, 15min, Belsat)
- After this week's drone incursion, officials say Lithuania has to find a defence against drones quickly and can't wait years for one. Lithuania is also sending equipment to protect Ukraine's power grid. (Delfi, Rubryka)
- Prosecutors have extended the freeze on former prime minister Saulius Skvernelis's apartment in Palanga. (LRT, 15min)
- An inquiry found that former ambassador to the US Gediminas Varvuolis may have misused embassy property, but bullying claims were not proven. (15min)
- President Nausėda discussed funding and security guarantees for Ukraine with European leaders in Dublin. (15min)
- Klaipėda University Hospital's owners will meet to discuss its money troubles. (15min)
